# One-tone and generative art: how the field does it

Benchmark for the Rueca poster series. Written 16 October 2026.

## How to read this

**What I checked.** Every claim carries a label:

- **[read]**: taken from a page's text. Most text came through a summariser (WebFetch), so treat it as a careful paraphrase, not a quotation.
- **[saw]**: I looked at the page in the built-in browser and described what was on screen. Where I saw it myself I say so.
- **[unverified]**: from a search snippet, a third-party write-up or memory. Do not cite it without opening the source.

**Limits.** Fourteen HEX case studies and the X article were read by me, and five of the cases I also looked at on screen. The other 30-odd peers were gathered by four research helpers and I spot-checked three (Figma Config 2026, Notion's campaign, Supabase Launch Week 15). My checks corrected two helper claims, noted where they occur. Studio sites rarely describe posters in text and the browser often returned blank art, so **poster scale, crop and grid are mostly [unverified] outside HEX and a few brands**. No colours or typefaces are named unless a page stated them.

**The headline finding.** Almost nobody does true one-ink generative art at poster scale. Most "one-tone" brands are one colour *ground* with a second accent, or black and white UI with a gradient hero. The verified one-tone devices are few: Supabase's stipple, Vercel's black-and-white pixel and hairline grid, Detail's sorted bars (HEX), First Star's plotted lines (HEX) and Exa's white linework on a single blue (HEX). **Nobody in the set uses engraving or hatching at poster scale.** That is Rueca's open ground, and also the reason there are few precedents for how to compose it.

## 1. HEX (hex.inc, @hellohex)

### Approach

Source: the X article "Moodboarding at HEX" (6 min read, posted 8 Oct 2026, 24.5K views), which loaded in the built-in browser **[read]**, and hex.inc **[read]**.

- A moodboard should pitch a **brand angle**, a specific world the client can react to ("we're pitching Alice in Wonderland for you"), not a curated Pinterest. The test: does the board connect the company's product to a belief and a feeling, or only to a look?
- "Visual consistency isn't a brand angle." Their example generic board (black backgrounds, fine lines, grids, technical diagrams, big type) looks like a style and says nothing about the company. This is a direct warning for Rueca: engraving plus a grid is a look until the series says something.
- Two worked angles. **Dessn**: Alice in Wonderland, rabbit hole, falling in as an invitation to explore, ending as flowers and a wormhole. **Synaptrix**: "Neural Universe", a NASA poster series for the brain, so people would want neural visualisations on their walls. Their line: it gets easier to build opinionated systems "when we have beliefs we hold very strongly."
- Studio facts **[read]**: led by Ayush Soni, a lean squad of animators, illustrators, copywriters and developers, 150+ projects, fixed 6 to 12 week brand projects. 14 "laws" including "Art is good; effective design is better."

**For Rueca:** the angle is already in the brand (Diderot's plates as the instruction manual for making tools). A poster series should state that angle in one sentence per poster and let the device follow, the way Synaptrix let NASA posters justify star maps.

### What HEX does with posters, social, type, grids and motion

Their pages carry little prose on this, so most of it is from what I saw.

- **Posters and cards sit at three scales.** (1) A huge headline set edge to edge along the bottom of a flat field, art behind it. First Star's "The State of Quantum" card [saw]: a flat blue field, tone-on-tone plotted contour rings behind a row of white nodes, small labels top left and right, headline spanning the full width at the bottom. (2) A small headline and a drawing in a blueprint panel. Exa's pair [saw]: ultramarine, a white wireframe temple at the bottom of the left panel, a serif headline at top left, a small button chip, an orbit diagram on the right panel with a serif statement above it and a row of small tick marks along the top edge. (3) A narrow vertical card with a UI window pasted over the device. Detail's black card [saw]: sorted white bars filling the lower half, sans headline top left, a small framed UI box overlapping.
- **Type.** Two voices on most work: a large grotesque or a serif for the headline, small monospace labels for metadata. Atomic Tessellator's wordmark is a serif set in capitals beside a node mark [saw]. Grafana uses a mono label on a coloured chip as a callout ("ANALYZE :: LGMT+ STACK") [saw]. First Star pairs a big grotesque headline with a serif for body inside the cards [saw]. No type names were published.
- **Grids.** Visible but never heavy: dashed hairlines with small corner squares (Atomic Tessellator, Grafana) [saw], 16:9 slide panels across the portfolio [read], a perspective grid at Moonlake [read].
- **Motion.** HEX's motion is procedural and loops: plotted keyframes (First Star), scanning lines (Grafana), fusing nodes (Sim), nodes and organic forms (Inference, Anthrogen) [read]. Showreels exist for several cases.
- **Premium cues** [my reading of what I saw]: very flat colour fields, one drawing language per client, small text kept small, tone-on-tone for the plotted backdrop so the white headline owns the contrast, and the device cropped by the card edge instead of floating.

### What to steal from HEX, what to avoid

- **Steal 1: a headline that spans the full width and sits on the bottom edge**, with the plotted device behind it tone-on-tone (First Star). Rueca's Goudy at weight 400 on a Mora field would do this with no new device.
- **Steal 2: the Exa blueprint panel.** Single ground, white line, a serif headline top left. It is closer to Rueca than anything else in the field because the drawing is of an old object (a temple) done as a technical drawing.
- **Steal 3: Detail's bars as an honest tone map.** The red marker bar is the only colour; the device explains the product. Rueca equivalent: engraved hatching that gets finer where the "tool" is finished.
- **Avoid: Composio's glow and beams and Grafana's multi-neon blocks.** Both break "lines stay lines" and the one-bright rule. Avoid HEX's habit of one device for everything: Grafana's block works because it maps to the product, as the article says.
- **Avoid copying "a look" without an angle.** The article's own warning applies to a poster series made of engravings only because they look good.

## 2. SaaS and product brands

Order: strongest evidence first.

### Supabase Launch Weeks

- **URL:** https://supabase.com/launch-week (Launch Week 15, 14 to 18 July). **[saw]**, confirmed by me in the browser.
- **Device:** stipple or particle fields of fine white dots on near-black. The hero is the word **"LW", then a wide strip of dense stipple, then "15"**, set in a large light sans: the art is the middle of the word. Below it, tall portrait cards with the same stipple in faint, sparse form, the date and title in plain sans bottom left **[saw]**.
- **One tone?** Yes in this edition: white on black. Earlier editions used purple gradients **[unverified, Dribbble tags]**. The page needs scroll for the art to load, which leaves a blank card at first paint **[saw by helper]**.
- **Placement:** art cropped hard into tall cards; density falls away toward one corner so the type sits in the quiet area.
- **Why it feels premium:** density does the tonal work with no outline, and the title sits in the empty part.
- **Steal:** art as one element inside a word ("PLATE ▒▒▒ 07"). **Avoid:** art that depends on scroll or animation to appear; a printed poster has one chance.

### Figma Config

- **URLs:** https://www.figma.com/blog/the-visual-identity-behind-config-2026 and the 2025 and 2024 posts; https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/figma-config-2024-identity-graphic-design-spotlight-090724. **[read]**, 2026 post re-read by me.
- **Device:** a **dithering tool** built so hundreds of speaker headshots shared one fine-scale "pointillism" treatment (Deanna Sperrazza's word). The opening keynote used blurred shapes and gritty dots. Shared Frequency: a kiosk where two people's hands generated a dot print, black and white dots on clear acetate laid over a base. 2025 used animated glyphs from primitive shapes, riso posters and blind embossing. 2024 used grown supergraphic shapes.
- **One tone?** The identity overall is multicolour. One-tone pockets: the acetate dot print and the 2025 blind emboss.
- **Placement:** the 2026 posters hung on a bright blue wall, "Config San Francisco 2026" with abstract graphics **[read]**. No layout or grid rules are published. (Correction to a helper: the 2026 post does not mention riso; riso is 2025.)
- **Why it feels premium:** the treatment is a **tool every image passes through**, and the physical materials vary (acetate, riso, emboss). The opening film was dropped from 60 to 15 fps to feel handmade **[read]**.
- **Steal:** make one small generator that every plate runs through, and print one piece on a physical material. **Avoid:** the multicolour glyph sprawl; Rueca's series needs a single ink.

### Vercel (Ship, Geist)

- **URLs:** https://vercel.com/ship (Ship 26), https://vercel.com/font?type=pixel **[saw by helper]**; https://vercel.com/blog/designing-and-building-the-vercel-ship-conference-platform and vercel.com/geist/introduction **[read]**.
- **Device:** Ship 2024 was "magnetic particles", Ship 2025 a ray-marched, near-metallic liquid; Vercel says it pulled back from the loud, shiny versions **[read]**. The current Ship page is pure black with a white wordmark, hairline-ruled cells and small uppercase mono captions **[saw by helper]**. Geist Pixel is a type family with five pixel shape variants on one axis; the Geist page calls the grid "a core part of the Vercel aesthetic" and cites Swiss design **[read]**. Giant pixel type sits on a faint ruled grid with crosshair registration marks and measurement tags **[saw by helper]**.
- **One tone?** The Ship 26 page and Geist Pixel page are white on black. The 2025 liquid is chrome. I could not see 2024 or 2025 posters or tickets.
- **Why it feels premium:** rigour: the grid and registration marks are the art. The tone is held to two values.
- **Steal:** crosshair marks and tiny measurement tags as plate-maker's marks. Rueca already has crosshairs in its sheets; put them on posters. **Avoid:** the 2025 liquid. Vercel itself called it too shiny and loud.

### Notion

- **URLs:** https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/buck-notion-graphic-design-illustration-project-170724 **[read, re-read by me]**; https://www.notion.com/blog/the-thinking-behind-our-latest-brand-campaign **[read by helper]**.
- **Device:** Roman Muradov's loose gestural line illustration, with Saul Steinberg named as an influence. The 2024 campaign by Buck adds **radial halftone for skin and linear halftone or drawn patterns for clothing**.
- **One tone?** The line is black. The campaign adds bold primary yellow, blue and red. Notion's own explanation: its earlier minimalist look was "getting lost" in global outdoor advertising **[read]**. (A helper reported the earlier work as strictly black and white; the Nice That article does not say so, so I have not.)
- **Placement:** close-up characters, enlarged and more distinct, bold headlines; sites include London Tube stations and Seoul bus stops **[read by helper]**.
- **Why it feels premium:** confident line, fine-art references, halftone as tone inside line.
- **Steal:** halftone as the tonal fill inside a line drawing, which is close to engraving's hatching. **Avoid:** fixing outdoor visibility by adding primaries. This is the clearest warning in the set for a one-ink series: solve it with scale, crop and paper contrast.

### Ramp

- **URLs:** https://ramp.com **[saw by helper]**; https://bakkenbaeck.com/case/ramp **[read]**.
- **Device:** a faint dotted grid across a cream page, dotted arcs of tiny dots sweeping through a feature card, handwritten marker annotations on a cut-out photo **[saw by helper]**. Bakken & Baeck describe a bento-box modular grid with sparse abstract imagery **[read]**.
- **One tone?** Mostly black on warm off-white with an acid yellow-green accent. The dot graphics are single-tone grey. Not strictly one ink.
- **Placement:** the dot fields sit behind or beside UI cards and never fight the headline, which is large, tight, black sans with generous margin.
- **Caution:** I found no page crediting Fons Mans with the illustrations. Do not cite that credit.
- **Steal:** dotted line fields (dots following an invisible curve) as a quiet secondary device, a gentle cousin of Rueca's dashed belt contours. **Avoid:** the novelty-photo wit; it is Ramp's voice.

### Stripe Sessions

- **URL:** https://stripesessions.com **[saw by helper]**.
- **Device:** a large, pale, translucent multicolour ribbon in a rounded card under a huge tight dark-aubergine headline; date and venue in small columns. Gradient, multi-colour.
- **Steal:** the date and venue columns as a tiny type block. **Avoid:** the glossy ribbon.

### Linear

### Anthropic (Claude)

## 3. Studios

Most studio sites gave text only, so these records are thin on layout. Read counts as case-study text.

### Studio Dumbar / DEPT: OpenAI DevDay 2025

- **URL:** https://www.studiodumbar.com/work/openai-devday-2025 **[read]**.
- **Device:** a type-only system from OpenAI Sans, with layout and motion drawn from code (indentation, function formatting), ASCII-style zoomorphic characters made of glyphs, and large square brackets framing the stage. D&AD wins per the listing.
- **One tone?** No. One screenshot by the helper showed orange, blue and green line work on black.
- **Steal:** make the image from the same glyph set as the text, so the poster has one voice. For Rueca: build the picture from the same hatching marks as the headline's "engraved text". **Avoid:** arcade and plush spin-offs.

### Pentagram: Oxide, Intrinsic

- **URLs:** https://www.pentagram.com/work/oxide/story, https://www.pentagram.com/work/intrinsic/story **[read]**.
- **Device:** Oxide uses an ASCII and TUI language, a slashed-zero "O", ASCII-style grid patterns, and a custom mono cut from Grilli Type extended with ASCII characters for real-time web illustration; the patterns are also laser-cut into server-rack panels. Intrinsic: generative robot-arm motion with echo trails.
- **One tone?** No. Oxide has green, red, blue and yellow on black; Intrinsic is flat bright on grey with gradient echoes.
- **Steal:** extend the typeface with pattern glyphs, so texture and text share one grid. **Avoid:** four accents on black.

### Wolff Olins: Lafayette Anticipations (2018)

- **URL:** https://typeroom.eu/article/lafayette-wolff-olins **[read]**; monochrome and "no imagery" from Dezeen-derived search snippets **[unverified]**.
- **Device:** a bespoke typeface with Colophon in which letters are cropped at random and an algorithm gives different letter combinations each time the same word is typed; the concept is concealing and revealing.
- **One tone?** Monochrome per snippets. The cleanest monochrome generative case in the studio set.
- **Steal:** one algorithm crops the letters, so a one-ink poster has surprise with no extra graphic. **Avoid:** relying on the randomness for legibility.

### Koto: Stack Overflow, Faculty

- **URLs:** https://www.creativeboom.com/branding/koto-reframes-stack-overflow-around-the-one-thing-ai-cant-replace-its-community/, https://bpando.org/2026/07/27/koto-stack-overflow-programmer-forum-rebrand/, https://www.creativereview.co.uk/koto-faculty-ai-branding/ **[read]**.

- **One tone?** Stack Overflow is orange-led with accents; Faculty uses gradients.
- **Steal:** one structural idea (stacking) drives hierarchy, layout and motion, and a tool keeps variants inside the rules. **Avoid:** gradients and multi-accent palettes.

### DIA: smlXL

- **URL:** https://www.the-brandidentity.com/project/start-in-motion-and-work-back-to-static-dia-create-a-generative-system-for-tech-start-up-smlxl **[read]**.

### Order

- **URL:** https://order.design **[read]**.

### Porto Rocha: Hiro

### Studio Lowrie: Sundance 2020

- **URL:** https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/studio-lowrie-sundance-film-festival-2020-identity-graphic-design-240320 **[read]**.
- **Device:** a solid black circle built from lines converging on a point, plus seven supporting symbols that tessellate in motion. A fixed kit, not algorithmic.

### Ragged Edge: Camden Market

- **URL:** https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/camden-market-rebrand-ragged-edge-031116 **[read]**.

### Two Times Elliott: Artah

## 5. What the field says about Rueca's posters

1. **The gap is real.** No peer in this set uses engraving or hatching at poster scale. Nearest cousins: Notion (halftone inside line), Mercury (fine radial lines), Supabase (stipple), HEX's Exa (temple blueprint), First Star (plotted lines). A one-ink copperplate series would be distinctive, but it has no benchmark for how to compose, so the composition has to be good enough to carry it.
2. **The premium cues are structural, not decorative:** a tool every image passes through (Config, DIA, Koto), a device tied to the product or brand parts (Grafana, Detail, Ragged Edge), hairlines and registration marks (Vercel, HEX), and type doing the work when art is plural (Mucho).
3. **The common failures:** gradients and glossy 3D (Vercel 2025, Stripe, Raycast, Faculty), multi-accent palettes on black (Oxide, DevDay, Composio), and one-tone art that disappears outdoors (Notion's own admission).
4. **Strongest single move to copy:** make a small generator for the engraving device, so every poster is a render of the same rule, and choose the best frames after the fact (DIA, Config).

## 6. Bar to clear: ten checks for the poster series

Judge each poster and the set together. A poster that fails more than two should be redrawn, not tweaked.

1. **One-ink test.** Convert to Nogal on Lana. It must still read, with every element present. In colour: Achiote appears once as one flat block, plus at most two accents, and no accent is a background.
2. **One focal device, with a quiet area.** The engraving or stipple occupies one clear zone, either 50 to 70 per cent of the sheet or a strip within a word. Its density falls away toward one corner, and the title sits in that quiet area (Supabase, First Star).
3. **Crop, not float.** The device bleeds off at least one edge or sits inside a plate frame. No evenly spaced object in the middle with equal margins on four sides.
4. **Type has two voices and a clear ratio.** Sorts Mill Goudy for the display line, IBM Plex Sans for everything else. The largest line is at least eight times the smallest. No more than three sizes. Weight 400, never bold.
5. **A declared grid with working marks.** State the grid (columns and baseline) and show at least crosshairs or tick marks. Every element snaps to it, and the marks are measured, not decorative (Vercel, HEX Atomic Tessellator).
6. **Line and texture discipline.** Three stroke weights only (object 1.4, detail 0.8, hairline 0.5). Stipple or dither at one dot size, or one deliberate density curve. No blur, no soft edge, no gradient, no glow. It must survive print at poster size and a 1080 pixel social crop.
7. **Series consistency.** Five constants never change (ground rule, grid, type pair, mark position, caption block). Only the plate and the title change. Remove the logo: it should still be recognisably Rueca (PostHog's test).
8. **System logic.** Each poster states one reason in a sentence, an angle in HEX's sense ("the wheel is a pencil"). The device derives from the logo's own parts (wheel, whorl, band) or from an engraved plate, and the generator's parameters are recorded so a poster can be rebuilt (Ragged Edge, Config).
9. **Restraint.** At most seven visible elements (art, title, three small labels, mark, one block). At least 30 per cent of the sheet is empty. No decorative frame inside the plate frame.
10. **Distance and thumbnail legibility.** At 200 pixels wide, the subject and the title still read. Contrast between art and paper is checked at that size, not fixed with extra colour (Notion's lesson). One poster is proofed on a real material (paper, acetate or emboss), because the field's best work is physical (Config).

## 7. Three compositions to borrow

Sizes are in a 3:4 portrait sheet (for example 1200 by 1600) with a 64 unit margin. Adjust for A-series prints.

### A. "The plotted plate": from First Star and Supabase

Borrows First Star's full-width bottom headline over a tone-on-tone plotted field and Supabase's density fall-off.

- **Ground:** Mora (the plum) flat, edge to edge.
- **Art:** concentric contour rings radiating from the drive wheel's centre, placed low and right so the centre sits at about 70 per cent across and 60 per cent down. Rings are Lana hairlines, 0.5 weight, spacing growing slightly each ring (the same offset curve the belt dashes use). The rings break into dashes as they near the top left, leaving that corner almost empty.
- **Type:** the title in Goudy 400, Lana, set in two lines, **sized so the longer line spans margin to margin**, with the second line's baseline on the bottom margin. Tracking minus 0.02 em. In the top margin, two Plex Sans labels, 22 units: series name left, date right. Between them, a row of small nodes on one hairline, as in First Star's card, for the poster number.
- **Accent:** one Achiote square (96 units) on the top right, sitting on the grid line, nothing else coloured.
- **Why it works:** a flat ground, one device, a headline that owns the contrast, and a quiet top-left to rest the eye.
- **Check:** the series changes only the ring centre (a different part of the wheel) and the title.

### B. "The blueprint diptych": from Exa and Atomic Tessellator

Borrows Exa's pair of ultramarine panels and Atomic Tessellator's dashed construction frame.

- **Format:** two portrait panels side by side with a 32 unit gap, shown as one social image and as two posters in print.
- **Ground:** Lana paper both panels. All line is Nogal. (Exa uses white line on blue; the Rueca version is the same idea in ink on paper.)
- **Left panel:** the engraved Saxony wheel at 78 per cent of panel height, bleeding off the bottom edge, drawn as a Diderot plate with hatched shadow, no floor. Over it, one Achiote 0.8-weight leader line to a lettered part "A". Above it, the title in Goudy 400, top left, three lines, left-aligned to the first column. A small Plex caption, "Fig. 07", under the title.
- **Right panel:** the construction view of the same wheel as pure line: circles, tangent band, dimension marks in a neutral measuring pen, dashed hidden edges. Above, a short statement in Goudy italic, six words at most. Along the top edge, a single row of tick marks as a ruler, copying Exa's tick row.
- **Frame:** a dashed rectangle with four small corner squares drawn around both panels together (Atomic Tessellator's frame), at 24 units inside the sheet edge.
- **Why it works:** the left panel shows what the thing is (engraving), the right shows how it is built (line), which is the existing Rueca pairing rule. The one bright is the leader line.

### C. "The word with a gap": from Supabase Launch Week and Detail

Borrows Supabase's "LW ▒▒▒ 15" lockup and Detail's sorted-bar tone map.

- **Ground:** Lana paper, Nogal ink.
- **Art:** a single horizontal band across the full width, 360 units high, vertically centred at 42 per cent of the sheet height. It is a tone map made of vertical engraved strokes (Detail's bars, drawn as hatching) whose spacing sorts from coarse on the left to fine on the right, so the band darkens toward the right. One stroke, taller than the rest and in Achiote, marks the "finished" part.
- **Type:** the poster's name is split by the band: the first word in Goudy 300 at 340 units to the left of the band's start, the numeral or second word to the right, both on the band's centre line and set in Nogal, so the band reads as the middle of the word, as in Supabase's "LW ▒ 15". The band is the same cap-height as the type.
- **Lower third:** a three-column block in Plex Sans 22 units, hanging from a hairline: date and place, a one-line description, a short URL. Columns are on the declared grid. Nothing else on the sheet.
- **Quiet area:** the top third is empty paper except the small crosshair marks in each corner.
- **Why it works:** it shows sort order, which is the product's promise (a rough prototype turning into a finished tool), in one image, with one bright and no effects.
